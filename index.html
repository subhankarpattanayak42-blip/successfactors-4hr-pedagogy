01 · Why this course

Students remember what they make.

Built on the pedagogy I run every weekend at Silicon University — MG3003 Enterprise HRIS (17 engineering undergrads, Sat–Sun): one idea per sprint, one thing each team builds, one page of output. Every HR term is translated on the spot for non-HR students.

A

For MBA / BBA / HRM

First contact with an enterprise HR suite — lifecycle to analytics in one sitting, with a story to hang it on.

B

For engineers / MCA

Systems thinkers who don't know HR: Employee Central becomes "the single attendance register the whole company shares."

C

No prerequisites

No acronym left unexplained. Position = an empty school bench; Job = the generic title; effective dating = version-history with dates.

02 · Learning objectives

By hour four, each student can…

1

Explain the landscape

What SAP SuccessFactors is and where it sits — vs. on-premise SAP HCM, Workday, Oracle HCM.

2

Navigate Employee Central

User, employee profile, org/job structures, and effective-dated history — version-history, never overwrite.

3

Run a talent flow

Requisition → offer → onboarding → goal → review, end-to-end in a guided sandbox/simulation.

4

Read people analytics

Headcount, attrition, 9-box — and state one decision each number supports.

5

Critique AI-in-HR

Screening, bias, privacy — in one graded paragraph. The exit ticket.

✓

Make, then name

Students DO the workflow first (click / configure / fill), then get the official SAP name. The hand moves first — the concept sticks.

03 · The 4-hour flow

Four blocks. Three artifacts. Zero dead air.

Fictional GlobalTech Inc. grew 300 → 500 people in a year. Payroll broke twice, offer letters go out from three inboxes, nobody knows headcount. The CEO gives your teams four weeks. Minute-by-minute facilitator script is in the repo.

0:00–0:55 A · Foundations — why cloud HCM, why SuccessFactors

Hook story → HR lifecycle in 10 minutes → suite tour (EC, Recruiting, Onboarding, PM/GM, LMS, Analytics) → live navigation demo of Employee Central. Teams form in the last 5 minutes.

Students make: annotated suite map — which module owns which HR moment.

0:55–1:05 Break

1:05–2:00 B · Core — the Employee Central data model

Org chart as folder tree · position vs. job · effective dating with a live version-history demo (hire → transfer → correction). "Never overwrite — always keep the date." Then Sprint 1 in teams with a fill-in-the-blank template.

Sprint 1 artifact: mini-org for one department + 3 effective-dated records.

2:00–2:55 C · Talent flow — hire to perform

Role-play: requisition → candidate → offer → onboarding checklist → goal sheet → review form. Volunteers play hiring manager, candidate, HR ops. Then Sprint 2.

Sprint 2 artifact: one completed hire-to-goal packet for a sample role.

2:55–3:05 Break

3:05–4:00 D · Analytics + AI + close

Read one headcount table, one attrition slice, one 9-box ("state one decision each number supports"). Live AI demo — screens 5 résumés in 30 seconds — then the debate: "Who owns a wrong AI rejection at 2 a.m.?" Room goes silent for the exit ticket. One-line takeaway round to close.

Sprint 3 + grade: 9-box placement with one-line justification + exit-ticket paragraph ("Should GlobalTech let AI reject candidates? Who owns the call?").

05 · Assessment

Light, honest, fair at sixty-plus.

40%

Participation quality

Reasoned team vote + handling one objection with evidence. Scored per team from a running note sheet — not memory.

40%

Exit-ticket paragraph

The grade. A position a manager could act on Monday: AI + hiring, with a named human owner. Marked in one pass, 2–3 min each.

20%

Peer question

Each team poses one sharp, answerable question to another team's artifact. Modelled once by the facilitator.
