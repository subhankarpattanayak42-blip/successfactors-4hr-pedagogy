# Assessment Rubric — SuccessFactors in 4 Hours

Total = 100. No phantom components; weights reallocated to real work; single-pass marking.

## 1. Participation quality — 40%

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 32–40 | Reasoned team vote + handled one objection with evidence; built on another team's point |
| 22–31 | Contributed a clear reason or a worked artifact; engaged in debate |
| 10–21 | Present, minimal contribution (one short comment, no reasoning) |
| 0–9 | Silent or disruptive |

Scored per team with individual adjustment: facilitator notes 1–2 standout/quiet names per team during sprints.

## 2. Exit-ticket paragraph — 40% (the grade)

Prompt: *"Should GlobalTech let AI reject candidates? Why / why not — and who owns the call?"*

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 32–40 | Takes a position; gives one concrete reason (bias, accuracy, law, trust); names an owner (human role, not "the model"); a manager could act on it Monday |
| 22–31 | Position + reason, but owner vague or action unclear |
| 10–21 | Fence-sitting summary with no position or no reason |
| 0–9 | Off-topic, blank, or single sentence |

## 3. Peer question — 20%

Each team poses one question to another team's artifact.

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 16–20 | Sharp, specific, answerable ("What happens to your transfer record if payroll already ran?") |
| 10–15 | Relevant but generic ("Why did you choose this structure?") |
| 0–9 | Missing, trivial, or hostile |

## Fair-at-scale notes (60+ rooms)

- Mark exit tickets in one pass, sorted by team; 2–3 minutes each.
- Participation scored from the facilitator's running note sheet (team × evidence), not memory.
- No curve-fitting: bands are absolute. Report distribution to the host professor within 48 hours.
