# SAP SuccessFactors in 4 Hours — Teaching Pedagogy

**A simulation-driven, learn-and-enjoy crash course on cloud HCM**
**Author:** Subhankar Pattanayak — AI Strategist, SAP · Instructor, MG3003 Enterprise HRIS (Silicon University) · FPM Scholar, IMI Bhubaneswar
**Contact:** subu@techsambad.com · techsambad.com
**Format:** 4 hours, single session (with 2 × 10-min breaks) · 30–60 students · No prerequisites

---

## 1. Why this course

Most HR-tech teaching stops at slides *about* SuccessFactors. Students remember almost nothing a week later. This 4-hour design flips that: students **play consultants in a live simulated company**, configure SuccessFactors concepts hands-on, and leave having **made three real artifacts** — not just notes.

It is built on the pedagogy I run every weekend at Silicon University (MG3003, 17 engineering undergrads, Sat–Sun 4–6 PM): *one idea per sprint, one thing each team builds, one page of output.*

## 2. Who it is for

- MBA / BBA / HRM / Business Analytics students meeting an enterprise HR suite for the first time
- Engineering / MCA students who understand systems but not HR processes
- **No prerequisites.** Every HR term is translated on the spot (e.g. *Employee Central = the single attendance register the whole company shares; Position = an empty school bench, Job = the generic title*).

## 3. Learning objectives

By the end of 4 hours, each student will be able to:

1. **Explain** what SAP SuccessFactors is and where it sits in the HRIS/HCM landscape (vs. on-premise SAP HCM, Workday, Oracle HCM).
2. **Navigate** the Employee Central data model — User, Employee Profile, Org/Job structures, and effective-dated history (version-history, never overwrite).
3. **Run** one end-to-end talent flow — Requisition → Offer → Onboarding → Goal → Performance review — in a guided sandbox/simulation.
4. **Read** one people-analytics output (headcount, attrition, 9-box) and state one decision it supports.
5. **Critique** an AI-in-HR scenario (screening, bias, privacy) in one paragraph — the graded exit ticket.

## 4. Pedagogy (how it teaches, not just what)

| Principle | What it looks like in the room |
|---|---|
| **Simulation, not lecture** | Fictional *GlobalTech Inc.* hires 200 people in 4 weeks. Every concept is a decision the students must take as consultants. |
| **One idea per sprint** | 3 team sprints × 1 crisp artifact each. Templates + everyday analogies provided; blank-page fear removed. |
| **Plain words first** | No acronym left unexplained. ERD = "a simple map with a scary name." Effective dating = "document version-history with dates." |
| **Make, then name** | Students DO the workflow first (click/configure/fill), then I give it the official SAP name. Concept sticks because the hand moved first. |
| **No exam; writing is the grade** | 40% participation quality + 40% exit-ticket paragraph + 20% peer question. Fair at 60+ strength (see Assessment). |
| **AI used honestly** | Live AI demo (resume screen / JD generator) followed by a bias-and-ownership debate. "Who owns a wrong AI shortlist at 2 a.m.?" |

## 5. The 4-hour flow

| Block | Time | Module | Students make |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 0:00–0:55 | **Foundations: why cloud HCM, why SuccessFactors** — HR lifecycle in 10 min, SuccessFactors suite tour (EC, Recruiting, Onboarding, PM/GM, LMS, People Analytics), live navigation demo | Annotated suite map (which module owns which HR moment) |
| ☕ | 0:55–1:05 | *Break* | |
| **B** | 1:05–2:00 | **Core: Employee Central data model** — org chart = folder tree, position vs job, effective dating with a live version-history demo | **Sprint 1 artifact:** mini org + 3 effective-dated records (hire, transfer, correction) |
| **C** | 2:00–2:55 | **Talent flow: hire-to-perform** — requisition → candidate → offer → onboarding checklist → goal sheet → review form; teams role-play candidate/manager/HR | **Sprint 2 artifact:** one completed hire-to-goal packet for a sample role |
| ☕ | 2:55–3:05 | *Break* | |
| **D** | 3:05–4:00 | **Analytics + AI + close** — headcount/attrition/9-box reading, AI screening demo + ethics debate, exit ticket | **Sprint 3 artifact + grade:** 9-box placement with one-line justification + exit-ticket paragraph ("Should GlobalTech let AI reject candidates? Why / why not, and who owns the call?") |

## 7. Assessment (light, fair at scale)

- **40%** participation quality (reasoned team vote + handling one objection)
- **40%** exit-ticket paragraph (would a manager act on it Monday?)
- **20%** peer question (each team poses one sharp question to another team's artifact)
- Top grade = paragraph an engineer could execute + a review prompt that explains its own honesty. No phantom components; single-pass marking.

Rubric detail: see [`docs/assessment-rubric.md`](docs/assessment-rubric.md).
